# Case Studies: Transferable Lessons

Use these to explain choices to the user and as a reference palette. Borrow principles, never content: don't reuse names, characters, plot turns, or recognizable set-pieces.

---

## Celeste (Maddy Makes Games, 2018)
**What it does:** A climbing game about a young woman with anxiety climbing a mountain. Opens with a single anxious line of inner monologue, then straight into movement. A shadow self appears early and argues she shouldn't climb. Chapters escalate difficulty as her doubt grows; she falls to the base before the final ascent. The last ability is a double dash that comes from reconciling with her shadow self; music themes merge at the summit.
**Levers used:** verb-as-metaphor (climbing = struggle), difficulty-as-emotion, optional side conversations that deepen side characters without blocking the critical path, invisible forgiveness mechanics (coyote time, buffering, corner correction), a mechanic, a musical and a story payoff all landing at the same beat.
**Steal this:** the final ability as the emotional resolution; side characters as mirrors of the protagonist's problem; forgiveness so the struggle feels fair.
**Don't copy:** mental-health-as-mountain is now a recognizable template; find *your* verb and *your* metaphor.

## Limbo (2010) & Inside (2016), Playdead
**What it does:** Wordless. Inside has no dialogue or text at all beyond the menu. Limbo's story is staging and silhouettes; Inside puts its story in the background layer and in the player's body (the marching sequence makes the story beat an input). Chases where escape is barely possible; sound and silence as the main tension tools; an ending that stays ambiguous.
**Levers used:** background events, crowds showing the world's rules, animation acting, restraint, a camera that moves with the protagonist's footing.
**Steal this:** "the verb is the story beat"; background-layer storytelling; chase tuning; silence before big moments.
**Don't copy:** ambiguity for its own sake. It works because every scene is concrete.

## Gris (Nomada Studio, 2018)
**What it does:** No dialogue, combat, or fail states. A grieving girl in a world drained of color; colors and abilities return stage by stage, commonly read as the five stages of grief. Each stage: find a new color/area, gain an ability, return to a central temple that becomes more complete. An ungainly block form, introduced in the anger stage, becomes a tool. The final level combines all abilities.
**Levers used:** palette-as-progress, hub-and-return, ability gain as healing, mechanics that reframe pain as useful.
**Steal this:** a written color script per chapter; a hub that visibly changes; "the painful mechanic becomes the coping tool".
**Don't copy:** relying only on beauty. Some players found the meaning obscure because everything must be inferred; plant enough concrete anchors.

## Hollow Knight (Team Cherry, 2017) & Silksong (2025)
**What it does:** Silent protagonist dropped into a ruined kingdom; story is implicit, delivered through environment, sparse NPC dialogue, item descriptions and fragments. Rewards the player's desire to explore with bits of story. Characters defined by action (a rival introduced through a fight). Silksong adds social strata visible in where and how inhabitants live.
**Levers used:** exploration-as-reward, architecture-as-history, boss fights as characterization, a large but cryptic lore layer for devoted players.
**Steal this:** fragments as exploration rewards; write each boss fight as a scene with a want.
**Don't copy:** cryptic lore depth requires a huge, handcrafted world; small games need a clearer critical path.

## Ori and the Blind Forest (Moon Studios, 2015)
**What it does:** An emotional prologue before the player has much control, then a movement-heavy metroidvania. Escape sequences after key areas (e.g. rising water in a tree) with dedicated music; players remember them as highlights, and also report them as the most frustrating parts when checkpoints are sparse.
**Levers used:** music-led set-pieces, early emotional investment, escapes that test mastered abilities.
**Steal this:** escape set-pieces as chapter climaxes, using only mastered abilities.
**Don't copy:** long one-mistake escape segments; checkpoint them. (Players note the sequel's chases were more forgiving.)

## Night in the Woods (Infinite Fall, 2017)
**What it does:** Adventure/platforming-lite with dense, funny, sad dialogue in wobbly speech bubbles. Writing is basically tweet-length; long thoughts are segmented, single-word bubbles used for humor and tension. Text-only (no voice acting). Branching is organic and tangent-prone; no Big Moral Choices. Choices are mostly about who you hang out with.
**Levers used:** voice-driven characters, rhythm in text, relationship-choice over moral-choice.
**Steal this:** bubble rhythm; one-word bubbles; "who do you spend time with" as branching.
**Note:** dialogue-heavy; for a platformer-first game, use its line craft, not its density.

## Oxenfree (Night School Studio, 2016)
**What it does:** Walk-and-talk: bubbles over characters, choices over the protagonist, the player keeps moving; can interrupt, wait, or stay silent. Built around "never have a cutscene". Known criticism: conversations outlasting the walkable space, forcing players to wait at screen edges or cut them off.
**Levers used:** dialogue as the core verb, silence as a choice, interruption as characterization.
**Steal this:** interrupt/silence mechanics; size spaces to conversations.
**Don't copy:** long talks in short rooms.

## Hades (Supergiant, 2020) — not a platformer, essential reference
**What it does:** Death advances the story. Dialogue is a bucket of conversations filtered by game state and weighted by importance; essential beats override everything; characters react to your weapon, recent death, and progress. Explicit goal: take the pain out of dying and restarting.
**Levers used:** reactivity, persistence across failure, many small conversations instead of few long ones.
**Steal this:** a small reactive-line system (death counts, last hazard, secrets found) for any platformer with retries or a hub.

## Quick lesson matrix

| Problem you have | Look at |
|---|---|
| Story feels separate from gameplay | Celeste, Gris |
| Want zero text | Inside, Limbo, Gris |
| Lore for explorers | Hollow Knight |
| Set-piece climaxes | Ori, Inside |
| Characters sound flat | Night in the Woods |
| Dialogue while moving | Oxenfree |
| Dying a lot feels pointless | Hades, Celeste |
