**The $1.4T figure is the most-misused number in this space.** It counts commerce transacted through iOS apps — Amazon orders, Uber rides, food delivery. It is not money available to app developers. Never present it as market size for a software product.

**Downloads are flat, revenue is growing.** Each install is worth more than it was. This changes strategy: acquisition volume is no longer the growth story, monetization per user is.

## The structural shift: non-gaming passed gaming

For the first time on record in 2025:

- Non-gaming in-app purchase revenue rose **21% YoY to roughly $85B**
- Gaming revenue grew roughly **1%**, at roughly $82B in IAP
- On the App Store specifically, ~$64.8B of $117.6B came from non-gaming apps

**The cause is AI.** ChatGPT's in-app purchase revenue grew 254% YoY to become the third highest-grossing app of 2025, accumulating 900M+ downloads between January and November 2025 and passing 1.3B total installs. AI-powered apps posted the strongest billings growth of any segment in the top 100.

This is the single most important strategic fact in the current market: **the growth is in non-gaming subscriptions, and disproportionately in AI-adjacent products.** It is also the most crowded place to enter — see `opportunity.md`.

## Category picture

**By downloads (share of all installs, 2023–24 baseline):** games ~41%, photo & video ~10%, shopping ~8%, entertainment ~7%, utilities ~7%, productivity ~6%, social networking ~6%, business ~4%, music ~4%.

**By revenue:** games and social networking dominate. Mobile games ~$196B / ~38% of total app revenue and social networking ~$153B / ~30% in 2024 per Newzoo, though these totals use a broader definition than store consumer spend.

**Non-game revenue leaders on iOS:** entertainment is the leading non-game category, with its iOS revenue share forecast to climb to ~13% by 2026. Entertainment services (Disney+, HBO Max and similar) generated ~$12.5B. Health and fitness grew 24% YoY to ~$6.3B.

**Fastest growing:** AI-powered apps across productivity, creativity and wellness — ~38% YoY install growth.

**Top grossing individual apps, 2025:** YouTube and TikTok each around $2.33B, Honor of Kings ~$1.87B, Monopoly Go! in the same tier. ChatGPT third overall by IAP revenue.

**visionOS** exceeded 4,200 AR/VR titles by Q1 2026 — early-stage, real but small.

## Store composition

| Metric | App Store |
|---|---|
| Free apps | ~94.8% |
| Paid apps | ~5.2%, and trending down (was 5.4%) |
| Games as share of apps | ~12.5% |
| Revenue mechanism split | IAP ~48% of mobile app revenue, paid downloads a much smaller and shrinking share |

**Paid-upfront apps are under 5% of the store and contribute even less of revenue.** In several categories the paid share is under 2% of listings — Business runs roughly 209,990 free to 2,026 paid; Food & Drink roughly 126,626 free to 1,649 paid; Health & Fitness roughly 126,729 free to 3,394 paid.

Where paid-upfront still works: professional and vertical tools, and game ports with an established name. Everywhere else the market has moved to free-with-conversion.

## Subscription benchmarks

From RevenueCat's State of Subscription Apps 2026 — the most useful dataset for an indie developer because it reflects actual app-level outcomes rather than store aggregates.

**Supply is exploding.** New subscription app launches went from ~2,000/month in January 2022 to **14,700+/month by January 2026** — a 7× increase. iOS accounts for ~77% of new subscription launches, up from ~67% in 2023. The steepest acceleration began in early 2025, coinciding with AI-assisted development tools, which default to App Store-first.

**But new ≠ successful.** Apps launched before 2020 still generate **69% of all subscription revenue.**

**Growth is bimodal.** Median YoY MRR growth is **5.3%**; the 90th percentile exceeds **306%**. Shrinking more than 33% puts you in the bottom quartile. There is very little middle.

**Conversion, by paywall model:**
| Model | Median D35 conversion |
|---|---|
| Hard paywall (trial → paid) | **10.7%** |
| Freemium | **2.1%** |

Roughly a 5× advantage to hard paywalls on conversion — though freemium wins on top-of-funnel volume, so the comparison is not decisive on its own.

**Conversion, by region (median D35 download-to-paid):**
| Region | Median | 90th percentile |
|---|---|---|
| North America | 2.6% | 10.4% |
| Western Europe | between | |
| India / SEA | 1.4% | |
| Latin America | ~1.5% | |

Conversion gaps between regions are narrower than revenue gaps.

**Value per payer (median year-1 realised LTV):**
| Region | Y1 RLTV per payer |
|---|---|
| North America | $32 |
| Western Europe | $25 |
| Global | $23 |
| India / SEA | $14 |

Türkiye is not broken out separately in the published summary but sits structurally closer to the emerging-market end. Treat NA/WE as where the money is.

**Other benchmarks in circulation (verify before citing):** productivity subscription apps averaging ~$47 ARPU, fitness ~$13, median free-to-paid conversion of 2–5%, and free trials converting at ~42.5% when onboarding is optimised.

## Retention and engagement

| Category | D1 | D7 | D30 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Gaming | 29–33% | ~16% | ~8.7% |
| Shopping/marketplace | ~33% | ~16% | 8–9% |

**71% of users churn within 90 days of installing.** Average daily app usage is ~3.6 hours globally; the average user touches 30+ apps per month.

Retention is the constraint that kills most indie apps, not acquisition. A subscription app with 8% D30 retention cannot support a monthly plan regardless of how good the paywall is.

## Regional revenue

| Region | Share of global app revenue |
|---|---|
| Asia-Pacific | ~33–40% (China drives ~60% of the region) |
| North America | ~30%; US forecast ~$86B consumer spend in 2026, US at ~42% of global App Store revenue |
| Europe | ~27%, growth in Germany and France |

Emerging markets — India, Brazil, Indonesia, Mexico, Türkiye — drive install growth, not revenue. This is the central tension for a developer in one of those markets: your local audience is reachable and cheap to acquire but does not pay; the audience that pays is elsewhere and expensive to reach.

## What these numbers mean for an indie developer

Five conclusions worth stating plainly:

1. **Subscription is the default model** and hard paywalls convert 5× better than freemium — but only if the product justifies a wall in the first thirty seconds.
2. **The market is saturating fast.** 14,700 new subscription apps a month against flat downloads means distribution, not development, is the binding constraint. AI made building cheap and did not make being found cheap.
3. **Non-gaming is where growth is**, and AI-adjacent non-gaming most of all — which is also where the 14,700/month is landing.
4. **Target NA/WE revenue regardless of where you live.** $32 vs $14 per payer is the difference between viable and not.
5. **Outcomes are bimodal, and age correlates with revenue.** Apps from before 2020 hold 69% of subscription revenue. A new app is fighting for the remaining 31% against 14,700 monthly entrants. This is not a reason not to build; it is a reason to have a distribution answer before writing code.
