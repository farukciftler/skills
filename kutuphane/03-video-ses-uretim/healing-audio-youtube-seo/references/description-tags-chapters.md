## 1. Field limits

| Field | Limit | Practical note |
|---|---|---|
| Description | 5,000 **bytes** | non-ASCII costs 2–4 bytes per character; a heavily decorated description hits the wall well before 5,000 visible characters |
| Above the fold | ~200 chars desktop, ~100–150 mobile | everything that must be read goes here |
| Tags | 500 chars total across all tags | 5–10 focused tags; not a ranking lever any more |
| Hashtags | 15 max; over 15 and all are ignored | 2–3 is the working number; first 3 from the description render above the title |
| Chapter title | 100 chars | shorter reads better in the player |
| Playlist title | 150 chars | truncates in feeds ~50–70 |
| Channel description | 1,000 chars | first ~100–150 shown in channel search results |

## 2. Description architecture

Order matters, because reading depth collapses fast:

1. **Above-fold block** — what this is, what it is for, how long (2–3 lines).
2. **How to use it** — the instruction that improves the session (volume, headphones or speakers, timer, screen off).
3. **Chapters** — timestamped, named.
4. **What you are hearing** — instrumentation, tuning, key, curation logic. This is the human-curation artefact.
5. **Credits and catalogue name** — track title, series, cover art, where else it lives.
6. **Keyword paragraph** — natural prose carrying the secondaries.
7. **Links** — DSP, other playlists, licensing if offered.
8. **Hashtags** — 3.
9. **Safety line.**

## 3. The above-fold block

The first ~100 characters are read by more people than the rest of the description combined, and they are what search snippets pull from. Put the primary phrase and the honest promise there, in prose, not as a keyword line.

Good:
> Eight hours of slow ney and low strings, tuned to A=432 Hz, for sleeping through the night. Black screen, no interruptions.

Bad:
> sleep music, 432 hz, relaxing music, meditation music, deep sleep, insomnia relief, spa music…

The second version is what most of this niche does, which is exactly why doing the first is a differentiator.

## 4. Chapters — the highest-value element in long-form

Chapters serve three purposes at once, which is rare: viewers navigate an eight-hour file, YouTube gets structural signal, and the named sections are visible evidence of curation rather than dumping — directly relevant to the inauthentic-content exposure in `claims-and-policy.md`.

Mechanics: the first timestamp must be `00:00`, you need at least three timestamps in ascending order, and each chapter should run at least ten seconds. For very long ambient files, chapter every 20–60 minutes.

Name them as **movements or scenes**, not as clock readings:

```
00:00 Settling
00:24 Rain arrives
01:10 Low strings, no melody
02:30 The quietest hour
04:00 Ney, distant
06:00 Drift
07:20 Fade to nothing
```

That list took two minutes to write and it is the difference between a curated release and an audio dump — to a viewer and to a reviewer.

## 5. The keyword paragraph

One short paragraph of real sentences carrying the two secondary phrases. Prose, not a comma-dump; it should read as if written for a person, because it is.

> People use this one for insomnia, for long night shifts, and as background for slow evening reading. If you prefer something brighter for daytime focus, the study playlist below runs at a slightly higher tempo.

Note what that also does: it sends the viewer to another playlist, which lengthens the session — a stronger ranking input than any tag.

## 6. Credits, catalogue name, links

State plainly: catalogue track name, series and number, tuning, whether the audio is instrumental, who made the cover, and where the release lives on DSPs. If licensing is offered, put the terms here — off-platform revenue is worth more than marginal RPM in this vertical and it also reads as a real business rather than an upload farm.

## 7. Tags in 2026

Tags carry minimal ranking weight now; title, description and viewer signals dominate. Spend two minutes, not twenty:

- 5–10 tags, 1–3 words each, inside the 500-character budget.
- Cover both orthographies of frequency tokens (`432 hz`, `432hz`).
- Include the lane, the instrument, the scene, the duration.
- Include a misspelling only if it is genuinely common (`solfegio`).
- Do not add competitor channel names.

Example: `sleep music, 432 hz, 432hz sleep, ney music, ambient sleep music, 8 hour sleep music, black screen sleep, insomnia music`

## 8. Hashtags

Two or three, placed in the description rather than the title so they do not consume title characters. The first three from the description render above the title, so choose them as billboard text: lane, spec, brand. `#sleepmusic #432hz #neyhours`. Never approach 15 — past that every hashtag is ignored, which is a self-inflicted wound for no gain.

## 9. Playlist and channel copy

Playlists are a ranking surface of their own and the main engine of session length in this vertical:

- Playlist titles are searchable: `8 Hour Sleep Music — 432 Hz Series` beats `My Sleep Tracks`.
- One playlist per lane, not one per upload. A viewer who lands on a sleep video should find seven more hours of the same lane.
- Sequence playlists so the tempo and brightness fall across the list — sleep playlists should get quieter, not shuffle into a bright piano piece at hour three.
- The channel description's first 100–150 characters do the same job as a title: what the channel is for, in the searched vocabulary.

## 10. Localisation

Use YouTube's localised title and description fields rather than stuffing two languages into 100 characters. Translate genuinely — a machine-mangled localised title reads as spam to that market's viewers. Localise the lanes where the demand actually is for the channel; three good localisations beat twelve careless ones.

## 11. Full template

```
[Above fold: 2–3 sentences. What it is, what it's for, duration, tuning.]

How to use it
[Volume, headphones or room speakers, sleep timer, screen off.]

Chapters
00:00 [named section]
00:24 [named section]
…

What you're hearing
[Instrumentation, tuning, key, tempo range, curation logic — why this was built this way.]

Release
[Catalogue name] · [Series] · [No.]
Cover art: [credit]
Also on: [DSP links]

[Keyword paragraph: 2–3 natural sentences carrying the secondary phrases, ending with a pointer to another playlist.]

Playlists
→ [Lane playlist]
→ [Adjacent lane playlist]

#lane #spec #brand

Not medical advice. Don't listen while driving or operating machinery. If you have a health condition, speak to a clinician.
```
