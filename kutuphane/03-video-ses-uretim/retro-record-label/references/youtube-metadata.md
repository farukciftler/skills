# YouTube & Release Metadata — Marketing Department

The job: an English-language metadata pack that a listener in Berlin, Manila or Chicago finds and clicks, without reading like it was written for a crawler. Written for a Turkish artist releasing globally, so the default output language is English with the Turkish identity kept as texture, not as a barrier.

## 1. Research first — the keyword landscape moves

Do not write from memory. The vocabulary listeners use for genres and use-cases turns over fast, and a 2022 phrasing wastes the title.

Run 3–6 `web_search` queries before writing:

- `"<genre> playlist 2026"` and `"best <genre> songs 2026"` — the words the audience actually uses for the genre right now.
- `"<mood> music for <activity>"` — use-case phrasing (study, driving, focus, coding, late night) is often a bigger door than genre for instrumental music.
- The genre plus a comparable artist, to see what surrounding language the results carry.
- Whatever niche descriptor the brief suggests (`"turkish psychedelic funk"`, `"anatolian rock revival"`) — check it is a phrase people use, not one you invented.

Pull out: **one primary keyword** (goes in the title), **three or four secondary keywords** (go in the description body), and **the format word** (Official Music Video / Official Audio / Lyric Video / Visualizer). Write them down before drafting.

For an artist with an existing catalogue, the artist's own name is the primary keyword and the genre is secondary — reverse the priority for a debut.

## 2. The title

**Pattern.** `Artist – Title (Format)` with an optional single qualifier. Consistency across a catalogue matters more than cleverness, because it teaches the algorithm and the listener the same shape.

```
Deniz Arslan – Night Ferry (Official Audio)
Deniz Arslan – Night Ferry | Turkish Psychedelic Funk
```

Rules that pay:

- **100 characters allowed; around 70 survive** in search results and far less on mobile and the Shorts shelf. Put the artist and title in the first 55–60 characters — weight and click decisions both live there.
- **One qualifier only.** "Official Music Video" *or* "Lyric Video", not both, and only if it is true. Mislabelling a static image as a music video costs retention.
- **A genre or mood clause after a pipe is legitimate** when the artist name has no pull yet, because it is the only place a discovery keyword fits naturally. Drop it once the name does the work.
- **Do not put hashtags in the title.** YouTube pulls the first three from the description and shows them above the title automatically.
- **Do not stuff.** The title's job is a promise; the description's job is context. A title that reads as a keyword list loses the click even when it ranks.
- **Match the thumbnail.** If they say different things, click-through drops.

Write two title variants and note which one you would ship. YouTube Studio can A/B test titles, and telling the user that is more useful than picking for them.

## 3. The description, above and below the fold

Roughly the first 150–160 characters show before "Show more", and that block is what search snippets and AI answer engines read. Everything above the fold has to work as a standalone sentence.

**Structure, in this order:**

```
[1] Hook + primary keyword, 1–2 sentences, ~150 chars.        ← above the fold
[2] Listen link (the smart link, one line).
[3] What this is: 2–4 sentences of real context.
[4] Chapters (if the track is over ~3 min or it's a mix/album).
[5] Credits block.
[6] Lyrics (if a lyric video) or the story behind it.
[7] Follow block — one line per platform, no emoji clutter.
[8] Keyword paragraph — 2–3 natural sentences, not a list.
[9] 3–5 hashtags.
```

What each part actually needs:

**[1] Hook.** Name the artist, the track, and one concrete thing about the sound. `"Night Ferry" is the new single from Istanbul producer Deniz Arslan — analogue synth bass, 1970s Anatolian funk drums, recorded to tape.` That sentence contains the primary keyword, reads like a human wrote it, and gives an answer engine something to quote.

**[3] Context.** Where it was made, what it was made with, what it is for. Instruments and gear are searchable and they signal authenticity. Two sentences beat six.

**[5] Credits.** Written, performed, produced, mixed, mastered, artwork. Real names, correctly spelled. This is where collaborators find themselves and where the release looks professional.

**[8] Keyword paragraph.** The one place where secondary keywords earn their space. Write sentences, not a comma list: *"If you're looking for Turkish psychedelic funk, Anatolian rock, or instrumental music for late-night driving, this is that. Fans of Altın Gün and Khruangbin should find something here."* Comparable-artist mentions are legitimate and effective; inventing an endorsement is not.

**Descriptions land best between roughly 150 and 350 words of real prose** plus the structural blocks. Under 100 words wastes a ranking surface; over 700 words of padding reads as spam to a human and adds nothing.

**Never in a description:** fake stream counts, fake press quotes, "please subscribe" as the first line, walls of unrelated keywords, or a copyright claim you do not hold.

## 4. Tags and hashtags

**Tags** get 500 characters total across all of them, roughly 30 per tag. YouTube has said they carry little ranking weight now and mainly help with misspellings and disambiguation — so use 6–10 honest ones and stop:

```
deniz arslan, night ferry, turkish funk, anatolian psych, instrumental funk,
analog synth, 70s funk, turkish music 2026
```

Include one deliberate misspelling or alternate transliteration of the artist name if it has Turkish characters (`deniz arslan`, `denız arslan`) — that is the one case where tags still earn their keep.

**Hashtags:** 15 is the hard cap and going over makes YouTube ignore *all* of them, so stay well under. Use **3–5**, at the end of the description. The first three appear above the title, so order them: broadest recognisable genre first, then the specific, then the artist.

```
#turkishfunk #anatolianpsych #denizarslan
```

Do not use the same five hashtags on every upload — vary the middle one by track.

## 5. Chapters and timestamps

Chapters need a `0:00` first entry, at least three entries, and each segment at least 10 seconds. They earn their place in three cases:

- **Albums and mixes uploaded as one video** — mandatory, one chapter per track with the track title, because those chapter titles are indexed text and Google surfaces them.
- **Long tracks** with distinct sections — name them descriptively (`2:14 Bass drops out`, not `Part 2`).
- **Behind-the-scenes or process videos.**

A three-minute single does not need chapters. Adding them to satisfy a checklist just eats description budget.

```
0:00 Intro
0:34 Verse
1:22 Chorus
2:40 Synth solo
3:58 Outro
```

## 6. Shorts and the second surface

A release is two uploads, not one. For the Short:

- Titles truncate to roughly 40 characters on the Shorts shelf — front-load harder than for long-form.
- Use the most distinctive 15–30 seconds, not the intro. The hook, or the strangest bar.
- Vertical crop of the cover art with the type still legible — this is where a cover designed to survive at thumbnail size pays off twice.
- Point at the full track in the description, once, without a hard sell.

## 7. The DSP metadata sheet

Deliver this alongside the YouTube pack. It is what the user pastes into DistroKid / CD Baby / Believe / FUGA. See `naming.md` §6 for the legality rules behind each field.

```
RELEASE
  Release title      Night Ferry
  Release type       Single
  Primary artist     Deniz Arslan
  Featured artists   —
  Primary genre      Funk
  Secondary genre    Psychedelic
  Language           Instrumental
  Release date       2026-09-18   (set ≥7 days out to allow editorial pitching)
  Territories        Worldwide
  Label              Bosphorus Sound (or "self-released")
  © / ℗ line         2026 Deniz Arslan

TRACK 1
  Track title        Night Ferry
  Version            —              (parentheses only, e.g. "(Radio Edit)")
  Explicit           No             (flag, never text in the title)
  ISRC               [from distributor]
  Composer(s)        Deniz Arslan   (full legal name, no aliases)
  Lyricist(s)        —
  Producer           Deniz Arslan
  Duration           3:41

ARTWORK
  3000×3000 px, sRGB, JPG, under 4 MB
  No URLs, handles, prices, platform logos, or explicit badges
```

## 8. Art tracks, Content ID, Official Artist Channel

Worth explaining once, because it confuses most independent artists:

- When a distributor delivers a release to YouTube Music, YouTube auto-generates an **art track** — the square cover on a topic channel, with "Provided to YouTube by …" in the description. That is separate from the video the artist uploads.
- Uploading the same audio to a personal channel *and* delivering it through a distributor can produce a **Content ID claim against the artist's own upload**. The fix is to whitelist the channel with the distributor before release, not to dispute afterwards.
- Consolidating the topic channel and the personal channel into an **Official Artist Channel** merges those surfaces and gives the artist the music-shelf layout. It requires a distributed release and is requested through the distributor or YouTube for Artists.
- Flag this to the user before release day rather than after the claim arrives.

## 9. Localisation without spam

Global reach does not mean writing the description in six languages.

- **English is the base.** One clean English description outperforms a multilingual pile.
- **Add a short Turkish paragraph at the end** if the artist has a domestic audience — clearly separated, three or four sentences, not a translation of the whole thing.
- **Use YouTube's translated-metadata feature** for a genuinely important market rather than pasting languages into one description field.
- **Place and language as texture, not translation.** "Recorded in Kadıköy, Istanbul" tells a foreign listener more than a translated poem does.

## 11. Field limits, one table

| Field | Limit | What matters |
|---|---|---|
| Video title | 100 chars | ~70 visible in search; front-load the first 55–60 |
| Description | 5,000 bytes | first ~150 chars above the fold; non-ASCII costs 2–4 bytes each |
| Tags | 500 chars total | ~30 per tag; low ranking weight, use 6–10 |
| Hashtags | 15 max | over 15 → all ignored; use 3–5, first 3 show above the title |
| Chapters | min 3, first at `0:00` | each segment ≥10 s |
| Channel description | 1,000 chars | first ~150 visible |
| Shorts title | 100 chars | ~40 visible on the shelf |
| Playlist title | 150 chars | one playlist per release era helps session time |

Emoji and Turkish characters consume extra bytes in the description budget — relevant only for very long descriptions, but worth knowing before a 4,900-character one gets truncated.
