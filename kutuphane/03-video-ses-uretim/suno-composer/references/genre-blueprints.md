# Genre Blueprints

Compositional skeletons, not prompts to paste. Each gives the musical decisions a competent arranger would default to in that lane. Start from the nearest one, then **deviate deliberately** — a blueprint used unchanged produces exactly the generic output the person is trying to escape. The deviation is the song.

Format: key/mode · tempo & feel · progression · form note · instrumentation · vocal · production.

---

## Pop / synth-pop

**Contemporary pop** — major or Dorian · 110–125, straight, four-on-the-floor or programmed backbeat · I–V–vi–IV, harmonic rhythm doubling in the pre · 16-bar chorus with an 8-bar post-chorus hook · programmed drums, sub-bass, pad, plucked synth or muted guitar · bright, polished, chorus in the upper tessitura · dense, loud, wide, radio-ready.

**80s synth-pop** — minor with a major-key chorus lift · 116–120, gated snare on 2 and 4 · vi–IV–I–V · big instrumental post-chorus · analog polysynth pads, Moog bass, DX-style bells, gated reverb drums · reverb-heavy, layered harmonies · bright, wide, unashamed reverb.

**Dark / minimal pop** — Aeolian or Phrygian · 68–80 with a half-time feel · i–VII–VI vamp, little resolution · space is the arrangement · sub-bass, sparse percussion, one processed texture, near-silence in the verse · breathy close-mic female voice, almost spoken · dry vocal against a huge low end.

## Rock / alternative

**Indie rock** — Mixolydian or major, guitar keys (E, A, D, G) · 128–140, straight eighths · I–bVII–IV or I–V–vi–IV · quiet verse / loud chorus, one guitar layer added at the chorus · jangly Telecaster, driving bass, live kit, occasional organ · raspy, slightly strained · roomy, mid-forward, not over-compressed.

**90s alt / grunge** — minor pentatonic, drop-tuned · 105–135, heavy backbeat, slight drag · i–bIII–bVII–IV, power chords · dynamic extremes between sections · thick distorted guitar wall, fuzz bass, hard-hit kit · half-sung, cracked, resigned then screamed · raw, dry drums, tape saturation.

**Post-rock / cinematic instrumental** — Lydian or Aeolian · 70–90, builds over 4+ minutes · slow harmonic rhythm, two chords for eight bars · through-composed crescendo, no chorus · clean delayed guitars, tremolo, strings, distant drums · instrumental · vast reverb, huge dynamic range.

## Hip-hop / R&B

**Boom bap** — minor, sampled feel · 88–94, swung, snare slightly late · looped 4-bar cycle, jazz seventh chords · 16-bar verses, 8-bar hooks · dusty drum break, upright or filtered bass, Rhodes or horn chop, vinyl crackle · conversational rap, off-the-beat placement · sampled, filtered, warm, deliberately lo-fi.

**Trap** — Aeolian or Phrygian · 130–150 with a half-time backbeat · single-chord or two-chord vamp, bass carries the movement · hook first, then verses · 808 sub with glides, rolling hi-hats and triplet fills, dark bell or pad · melodic rap, ad-libs, autotune (or exclude it deliberately) · sub-heavy, sparse mids.

**Neo-soul** — major with extensive borrowed chords, or Dorian · 72–84, laid-back, behind the beat · Imaj7–iii7–vi7–ii7, extended voicings throughout · loose form, extended outro vamp · Rhodes, warm round bass, brushed or loose live kit, muted trumpet · silky, runs and melisma, layered harmonies · warm analog, unhurried, wide stereo keys.

## Electronic

**Deep house** — minor 7ths, Dorian · 122–126, four-on-the-floor, shuffled hats · two-chord vamp, no cadence · 8-bar phrase building, breakdown at two-thirds · warm bass synth, Rhodes stabs, filtered vocal chops, crisp hats · chopped or wordless · spacious, filtered, hypnotic.

**Melodic techno** — Aeolian · 124–132, driving straight · i–VI or a single pedal-point drone · long builds, one release · analog arpeggio, sub, resonant filter sweeps, sparse percussion · minimal or none · dark, dry, relentless.

**Drum & bass** — minor · 172–176, breakbeat over a half-time-feeling sub · one or two chords, pad-driven · intro / drop / breakdown / second drop · chopped amen break, Reese bass, atmospheric pad · sparse vocal fragments · tight, punchy, sub-loaded.

**Lo-fi / chillhop** — major 7ths, F or C major · 70–82, swung, deliberately loose timing · Imaj7–vi7–ii7–V7 loop · seamless 8- or 16-bar loop, no dramatic arc · Rhodes, tape-saturated drums, upright bass, vinyl crackle, distant sample · none, or a pitched-down fragment · warm, filtered top end, tape hiss.

## Acoustic / folk / country

**Indie folk** — major or Dorian in D, G, C · 88–100, gentle straight eighths or 6/8 · I–V–vi–IV with a borrowed iv for the ache · verse–refrain, no big chorus · fingerpicked acoustic, upright bass, brushed snare, occasional harmonium or harmonica · soft, breathy, close-mic'd, doubled on the refrain · intimate, roomy, minimal compression.

**Country / Americana** — major, open guitar keys · 95–120, backbeat with a train shuffle option · I–IV–V with a V/V pushing into the chorus · strict verse–chorus, storytelling verses · acoustic and Telecaster, pedal steel, fiddle, brushed kit, walking bass · drawled, narrative, plain-spoken · dry, clear, mid-forward.

**Ballad** — major with a minor iv, or natural minor · 62–76, 6/8 or slow 4/4 · I–vi–IV–V, half-cadence into every chorus · big last chorus, possible whole-tone lift · piano, strings entering at the pre-chorus, brushed kit late · full range, restrained verse, belted chorus · wide, orchestral, generous reverb.

## Jazz

**Swing / standard** — Bb, Eb, F major · 120–200 swung, or 60–80 for a ballad · rhythm changes or ii–V–I chains, AABA 32 bars · head / solos / head · piano comping, walking upright, brushed or ride-led kit, tenor or trumpet · warm, behind-the-beat, conversational phrasing · mono-ish, close, room-mic'd, 1959 feel.

**Bossa nova** — major 7ths and 6ths, often A or D · 120–140 but felt at half that · Imaj7–ii7–V7 with chromatic passing chords · two-part form, understated · nylon guitar with bossa comping pattern, brushed kit, shaker, light bass · soft, almost whispered, unornamented · dry, close, intimate, small room.

## Turkish and Anatolian

Read `theory-toolkit.md` § 12 first for the makam-to-12-TET reality check. Instrumentation carries the identity more reliably than scale specification.

**Anadolu rock** — Hicaz/Phrygian dominant or Nihavend/Aeolian · 100–130, straight or 9/8 aksak (2+2+2+3) · i–VII–VI–V Andalusian descent, or a modal drone · long instrumental intro, riff-driven · electric bağlama or fuzz guitar doubling a saz line, organ, bass, live kit, darbuka layered under the kit · full-throated, ornamented, slightly nasal · 70s analog warmth, tape, roomy drums.

**Türkü / acoustic folk** — Uşşak or Hüseyni (will land Dorian), Kürdi (Phrygian) · 80–110, or free-tempo · modal, drone-based rather than functional harmony · free-tempo taksim intro then a metered body · bağlama/saz, ney, kaval, bendir, kanun · plaintive, heavily melismatic, ornamented · dry, close, single-room, no modern polish.

**Modern Turkish pop** — Nihavend/Aeolian with a Hicaz turn in the hook · 100–118 · vi–IV–I–V with the modal colour in the melody, not the chords · standard verse–chorus, big post-chorus instrumental hook · programmed drums plus real darbuka, string section, kanun or synth doubling the hook line · polished, powerful, ornamented on the phrase ends · contemporary, loud, wide.

**Arabesk-adjacent ballad** — Hicaz · 70–90, slow, rubato-leaning · i–VII–VI–V, descending · long orchestral intro, dramatic · string section, kanun, ney, ud, darbuka · full, aching, heavy melisma, dramatic dynamics · lush, reverberant, string-forward.

## Functional music

**Game loop** — the requirement is seamlessness and non-fatigue. 8, 16 or 32 bars that return cleanly to bar 1 · avoid a strong cadence at the loop point (it announces the seam); end on a half cadence or a sustained pedal · avoid distinctive one-off events (a cymbal crash, a vocal hook) — they become maddening on the fortieth pass · use a modal vamp rather than a functional progression · layer for intensity states if the game has them (exploration = pad + arpeggio; combat = add percussion and a low ostinato). Prompt it as instrumental, name the loop length, ask for a consistent dynamic level.

**Ad / brand bed** — 15, 30 or 60 seconds means the math matters: at 120 BPM, 30 seconds is exactly 15 bars in 4/4 · front-load the hook, resolve firmly at the end · leave a midrange hole for voiceover (say "sparse midrange, leaves room for narration") · one memorable 3–5 note motif, stated at least twice · bright, uncluttered, unambiguous mood.

**Podcast / video intro** — 5–15 seconds · one gesture: a rise, a statement, a landing · single strong cadence at the end · no vocals, 2–3 elements maximum.

**Cinematic trailer** — Aeolian, huge · 70–90 felt in half-time · i–VI–III–VII, one chord per two bars · relentless build in three tiers, braam, silence before the last hit · low strings ostinato, taiko, brass swells, choir, sound-design hits · instrumental or wordless choir · enormous, sub-heavy, cinematic reverb.
