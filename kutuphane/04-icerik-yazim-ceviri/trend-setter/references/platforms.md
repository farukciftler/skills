# Platform Playbooks (state of mid-2026)

Algorithm details drift monthly. The mechanics below are current as of mid-2026; when a deliverable depends on an exact number (character limits, feature availability, a specific ranking change), verify with a quick web search dated to the current month before asserting it to the user.

Universal law across every platform in 2026: **distribution is earned per-post, not per-follower.** Feeds moved from follow-graphs to interest-graphs. Content is embedded and understood semantically; the first 30–90 minutes of engagement velocity decides reach; dwell time, completion, saves, shares, and replies outweigh likes everywhere.

## 1. LinkedIn

**Ranking reality.** Since early 2026 the feed runs on an LLM-based ranking system that reads what a post is actually about and matches it against each member's interests — and against *your* profile. Topic-profile fit matters: posts inside your established expertise travel; random-topic posts stall. Hashtags are nearly irrelevant. Good posts resurface for weeks if engagement continues.

**What ranks:** dwell time (people actually reading), comments of 10+ words, saves and shares, replies you make within 1–2 hours, carousels/PDF documents (text-only posts on company pages carry a fraction of the reach). Personal profiles massively outperform company pages; treat the company page as an archive and people as the channel (employee posts, founder posts).

**Post anatomy:**
- Hook = first ~200 characters (what shows before "...see more"). It must work standalone.
- Body in 1–2 sentence paragraphs, lots of white space, no headers.
- One idea per post. 900–1.800 characters is the sweet spot for stories; short punch posts also work.
- External links: put them in the comments or at the end after real content — link-first posts get throttled.
- End with a genuine question or a clean punchline, not "Agree?"

**Cadence:** 2–5 posts/week for creators; never more than 1/day (posts cannibalize each other). Spend 15 minutes commenting on others before publishing — primes the network for your first-hour velocity. 80% value, 20% promotion; accounts that flip the ratio bleed reach.

## 2. X (Twitter)

**Ranking reality.** Since January 2026 the For You feed runs on a Grok-based transformer that reads posts and watches videos to match interest. Engagement weights are lopsided: a reply is worth roughly 27× a like, and a reply you answer (a conversation) is worth far more still. Velocity in the first 30–60 minutes is the strongest signal.

**What ranks:** native video (biggest format boost — even simple talking-head clips), conversation-starting text, long-form single posts (now favored over multi-post threads for distribution; use threads only when serialization genuinely helps the story), original images.

**What kills reach:** external links in the post (near-zero median engagement for non-Premium accounts — put the link in the first reply), engagement-bait that draws reports, posting into dead hours.

**Post anatomy:**
- The first line is the whole game; write it as a standalone hook.
- One claim or one scene per post. Lowercase openers are native in tech/creator niches — match the account's register.
- Hashtags: 0–1. They're for campaigns, not reach.
- Reply plan: answer replies fast for the first hour; conversations compound distribution.
- Premium is effectively table stakes for organic visibility on brand-critical accounts; flag this honestly when strategy depends on it.

## 3. Instagram

**Ranking reality.** Reels and carousels lead. Watch time and completion dominate for Reels; saves and DM-shares ("send to a friend") are the strongest growth signals overall. An originality scoring system suppresses recycled and watermarked clips — no TikTok/CapCut watermarks, no verbatim trend copies. Captions are searchable: Instagram is a search engine now, so put the topic keyword in the first caption line, not just in hashtags.

**Format guide:**
- **Reels:** hook inside 1–1.5 seconds (the 3-second rule is dead), design for sound-off with bold text overlays, drop viewers into the payoff frame first — no logo intros. Shortest length that delivers wins: a 10-second Reel at 80% retention beats 60 seconds at 30%. Loop-friendly endings earn replays, which the algorithm loves. Use trial reels to test hooks on non-followers before committing.
- **Carousels:** the save machine. 6–10 slides, hook slide + re-hook on slide 2, one idea per slide, CTA/discussion slide last. Design rules live in `design-system.md`.
- **Stories:** community maintenance, not reach — polls, questions, behind-the-scenes. Keep interactive stickers frequent.
- **Captions:** first line = keyword + hook; 3–5 hashtags max; add keyword-bearing alt text.

## 4. TikTok

**Ranking reality.** Completion rate is king — roughly 70%+ completion is the viral threshold in 2026, up from ~50% two years ago. Every video is scored on micro-retention: the swipe/stay decision at second 1, second 3, and every few seconds after. Search intent matters: spoken words are transcribed and indexed, so say the keyword out loud early. Comments, saves, shares, profile visits, and replays all feed distribution.

**Script anatomy (works for Reels/Shorts too):**
- 0–1s: visual + verbal + on-screen-text hook, all three at once
- 1–5s: re-hook — sharpen the promise or raise a stake
- 5–25s: payload in fast beats; cut every 1.5–3 seconds; captions on
- End: loop into the opening frame, or a question that farms real comments
- Length: whatever retention supports. 7–15s for single-idea clips; 60–90s for story-driven content (long-form is back — story videos hold retention surprisingly well).

**Practices:** film/export native (no cross-platform watermarks), use current sounds only after checking they're actually current (see `trend-research.md`), build series — episodic content trains return viewers, and platforms in 2026 reward serialized formats.

## 5. YouTube Shorts

Search-adjacent: titles and spoken keywords matter more than on TikTok. Loops earn replays; the first frame doubles as a thumbnail, so make it legible. Consistent series with a repeatable title pattern ("X in 60 seconds, ep. 12") builds subscriber conversion, which Shorts pushes harder than other platforms. Point viewers to a matching long-form video when one exists — the Shorts-to-long pipeline is the actual monetization path.

## 6. Facebook / Threads / Bluesky (brief)

- **Facebook:** actively suppresses unoriginal content patterns — cross-posting identical assets is penalized. Reels and Groups are where organic reach still lives. Older demographic skew; longer captions tolerated.
- **Threads:** conversational text-first; links are tolerated; the register is casual and slightly ironic. Good second home for X-style content, rewritten not cloned.
- **Bluesky:** small, high-engagement, community-norm-sensitive tech/creative audience. Native jokes and sincerity work; corporate voice dies instantly.

---

## 7. Cross-posting rules

Never clone. For each additional platform, re-make three things: the hook (platform register differs), the format (aspect ratio, length, text density), and the CTA (what natives actually do there). The efficient pipeline: one pillar piece (long video, deep post, or article) → 5–8 platform-native satellites. Meta and others detect duplicate patterns; even the same video should get platform-specific captions, cover frames, and opening seconds.

---

## 8. Cadence and timing

Sustainable beats optimal: a cadence the user can hold for 6 months outperforms a two-week sprint. Baseline guidance — LinkedIn 2–5/week, X 1–4/day for creators (less for brands), Instagram 3–5 Reels or carousels/week plus near-daily stories, TikTok 3–7/week. Timing: publish when the specific audience is awake and idle (for Türkiye B2B: weekday 08:30–10:00 and 12:00–13:30 are reliable starting hypotheses); then let the account's own analytics override generic advice within two weeks. Every post ships with a first-hour plan: be online, reply to everything, and seed one thoughtful comment thread.
