# Replacement bank

Read this on demand, not up front. It backs §8 (AI vocabulary), §13 (nominalization), §17 (prepositional pile-up), §37 (filler) and §48 (copula avoidance).

Two warnings before you use it. First, a swap table cannot do the work on its own; replacing words while keeping the sentence skeleton is exactly the failure §47 describes. Fix the shape first, then reach for this. Second, none of the left-hand column is forbidden English. Every one of these words is right somewhere. The tell is density and default reach, not presence.

## Adjectives to check

Not banned, but each one is doing less work than it looks. If you cannot say what it adds, cut it: comprehensive, holistic, robust, seamless, meticulous, intricate, nuanced, multifaceted, vibrant, bustling, cutting-edge, groundbreaking, transformative, innovative, pivotal, crucial, vital, invaluable, unparalleled, remarkable, compelling, profound, rich (figurative), diverse (as filler), key (as filler).

The reliable test is substitution: if the sentence means the same with the adjective deleted, delete it. If it means the same with the opposite adjective, the sentence has a bigger problem than the adjective.

## Idioms and figures

English AI prose is not short of idiom; it is short of *fresh* idiom, and it reaches for the same dead metaphors: moving the needle, low-hanging fruit, the tip of the iceberg, a double-edged sword, a perfect storm, at the end of the day, game changer, paradigm shift, think outside the box, take it to the next level, in the driver's seat, the elephant in the room.

Three rules. Use an idiom the way you use salt: at most one in a few hundred words, and never two side by side. Never mix two (the model produces hybrids that do not exist in English, and a native reader hears it immediately). Match the register: street idiom in a compliance document is as wrong as a proverb in a text message.

If you cannot find a live idiom, use a plain sentence. A plain sentence never sounds like a machine trying to sound human; a stale idiom often does.
